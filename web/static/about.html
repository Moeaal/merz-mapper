About the project

A robot that learns the building, so visitors do not get lost

Merz Mapper is a final-year student engineering project. A rover drives the corridors of Merz Court on its own, builds a map, reads the room numbers on the doors and takes a photo at every junction. This website turns that into directions you can follow on your phone.

What the rover does

The rover is a small wheeled robot built at the university. It carries a small computer and three sensors, and does four jobs on each trip through the building.

  1. Drives itself

    Starting at an entrance, it heads for the parts of the building it has not yet seen, and stops at doors it cannot open.

  2. Builds a map

    A spinning laser scanner (LIDAR) measures the distance to the walls many times a second. Joining these up gives a floor plan; the aim is for every wall to be within 15 cm of where it really is.

  3. Reads the door signs

    A camera spots room signs and reads the numbers. A depth camera works out where each sign is, so the room can be placed on the map.

  4. Photographs junctions

    Wherever corridors meet, it takes a photo. These are the pictures you match against what you see, step by step.

From the map to your directions

The QR poster at each entrance tells the website where you are. The website only offers rooms the rover could actually reach from that entrance: if a locked door was in the way, those rooms are left out, so you are never sent through a door you cannot open.

Your route is the shortest path along the corridors between junctions. The photo at each junction, with a turn arrow, shows which way to go next. Everything shown comes from the rover's own records; the website cannot change them.
